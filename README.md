 Brief: Digital Earth Africa (DE Africa)

1. Who are they?

DE Africa is a nonprofit initiative that gives African governments, researchers and communities free access to satellite data they can actually use. It launched in August 2019 with the aim of making satellite data freely accessible and decision-ready for Africa. After six years it hosts over 6 petabytes of satellite data and has more than 29,000 active users and 1,300 trained professionals. It works through African partners, including AFRIGIST in Nigeria, the Centre de Suivi Écologique in Senegal and the Sahara and Sahel Observatory in Tunisia. 

2. What data do they use?

The raw material is open satellite imagery from international space programs. DE Africa cleans it into "analysis ready" form, so users skip the slow work of downloading and organizing images. That is what makes it practical. A researcher in Accra can map a shoreline without a supercomputer. The data feeds services on coastlines, water, flooding, crops and land change. 


3. What changed because of it?

Ghana. Erosion along the Keta Lagoon was hard to track at scale. Researchers used DE Africa's tools to identify high-risk erosion zones and to check whether existing sea defences were working. The plan is an early-warning shoreline app for agencies such as the National Disaster Management Organisation. That app is still a goal, not a finished product. 


Nigeria. DE Africa reports that its flood models supported evacuations during the October 2024 Kogi State floods. For readers in Nigeria, this is the clearest example of satellite data helping before a disaster hits. 


Agriculture. DE Africa credits open satellite data with productivity gains valued at nearly USD 900 million. I'd treat that as the organization's own estimate, not an independent audit.
